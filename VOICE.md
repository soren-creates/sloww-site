# Sloww — Voice & Style Guide

How Sloww sounds, in marketing copy, the App Store listing, and the app itself.
Built on the brand's own register plus Ann Handley's *Everybody Writes*.

## The voice in one line

**Contemplative, concrete, and quietly confident** — a clock that respects your
attention, written the same way.

We sound like someone who watches the sky and tells you what they see, plainly.
Not a productivity app. Not a wellness brand. Not a startup.

## Principles

1. **Show, don't tell.** Never call the writing lyrical, warm, or thoughtful —
   *be* those things and let the reader notice. "Answers in sentences, not stats"
   beats "a warm, literary voice."

2. **Lead with the thing.** Put the strongest words first. State what it is before
   you sell it. Kill weak openers ("There is…", "We've built…", "Designed to…").

3. **Concrete over abstract.** Name the sun, the twilights, the 29.5-day cycle, the
   nightstand. Sensory and specific always wins over benefit-speak.

4. **Write for one person who's paying attention.** Assume they're smart and know
   nothing about us yet. Explain without condescending.

5. **Earn every word.** If a sentence survives deletion unmissed, delete it.
   Sentences under ~25 words. Short paragraphs, one idea each.

6. **Dry, not cute.** A little wry confidence is welcome ("There when you want it,
   quiet when you don't"). Whimsy, exclamation marks, and winking are not.

## Banned: the AI / SaaS tells

- **Adjective puffery** — *seamless, beautiful, thoughtful, delightful, powerful,
  lyrical, warm.* If you're reaching for an adjective to do the work, rewrite.
- **The persona rule-of-three** — "great for travelers, photographers, or anyone
  who…". Name a real moment instead.
- **Buzzwords & marketing filler** — *revolutionary, cutting-edge, value-added,
  seamless, elevate, unlock, supercharge, game-changing, "Always a glance away,"
  "No spam."*
- **Empty hype** — *included forever, the only app you'll ever need.*
- **Em-dash-and-restate cliché** — using "— it's that simple" as a crutch.

## Words & mechanics

- **Use** > utilize. **Show** > showcase. **About** > regarding.
- Active voice. The reader, the sun, the clock *do* things.
- Spelling: **US English** (math, color, favorites). One exception is fine if it's
  clearly intentional — otherwise default US.
- Numerals for data and time (5 rings, $4.99, 29.5-day). Spell out small counts in
  prose when it reads better ("seventeen voice intents").
- Em dash —, not hyphen, for breaks. `&mdash;` in HTML, `&nbsp;` to keep tight
  pairs together (Dark&nbsp;+&nbsp;Red).
- Title case for feature names (Night Mode, Celestial Bells, Settings Lock);
  sentence case for everything else.

## Voice in the wild

| Instead of… | Write… |
|---|---|
| Seventeen intents in a warm, literary register | Answers in sentences, not stats |
| Beautiful at every latitude | From the equator to the midnight sun |
| Handy for travelers, photographers, or anyone with people far away | The place you're headed next week, or the one where it's already tomorrow |
| Get gentle reminders so you never miss golden hour | A quiet chime at sunrise, sunset, golden hour, moonrise |
| No spam, ever | Nothing arrives that you didn't choose |

## Before you ship

1. **Read it out loud.** If you stumble or run out of breath, cut.
2. **Look at it.** Scan the shape on screen — wall of text means break it up.
3. **Find the adjective doing the selling** and replace it with a fact or an image.

> Draft ugly, then edit hard. The first version is for getting it down; the voice
> is found in the rewrite.
